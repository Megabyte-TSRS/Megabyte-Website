The session began with an interactive "AI or Real?" Kahoot, where members were shown a series of images and challenged to determine whether they were AI-generated or authentic photographs. After each round, the group discussed the visual cues that helped distinguish AI-generated content from real images, including inconsistencies in details, unnatural textures, lighting, and object relationships. The activity encouraged critical thinking about the growing capabilities of generative AI and the importance of evaluating digital media carefully.

Following the icebreaker, members were introduced to the AI project development cycle, with particular emphasis on the first and most important stage: problem scoping. The discussion focused on how successful AI solutions begin by understanding the underlying problem, identifying stakeholders, defining clear objectives, and considering real-world constraints before thinking about technology or implementation.

Students were then divided into teams of two or three members and challenged to apply these principles under the theme "Mindset of Sustainability" and "Future in Focus". Each team selected an environmental issue that they believed technology could help address, ranging from waste management and energy conservation to pollution and biodiversity. They brainstormed innovative solutions in the form of mobile applications, AI assistants, websites, games, robots, wearable devices, sensor systems, and other technology-driven ideas.

To conclude the session, each team created a short presentation and presented their proposed solution to the rest of the society. The activity encouraged collaborative problem-solving, creative ideation, and structured thinking while reinforcing the importance of beginning every technology project with a well-defined problem.
