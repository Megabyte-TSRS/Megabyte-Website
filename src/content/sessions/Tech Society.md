In this collaborative policy-design activity, society members assumed the role of the Governing Council of a newly established society in the year 2026. Working in groups, students were challenged to create one governing rule for each of five key domains: education, technology, the environment, money and jobs, and laws and freedom. Every rule was designed with the assumption that modern and emerging technologies would play a significant role in society, encouraging participants to think critically about the relationship between innovation, governance, and ethics.

Beyond proposing rules, each group identified a potential challenge or unintended consequence that could arise from one of their policies. This encouraged students to recognise that even well-designed systems involve trade-offs, requiring policymakers to balance fairness, sustainability, and technological progress while anticipating possible risks.

Following a period of discussion and preparation, each group presented its proposed framework to the society. Speakers explained the purpose of each rule, the technologies required for implementation, why the policy was considered fair, and how it would remain effective in the long term. Audience members actively questioned the proposals, challenged assumptions, and offered alternative perspectives, creating an engaging discussion on responsible governance and evidence-based decision-making.

The session concluded with a reflective discussion in which members considered the skills they had developed through Megabyte Society, misconceptions that had changed during the year, and suggestions for improving future sessions. This reflection reinforced the importance of systems thinking, collaboration, critical evaluation, and responsible technological decision-making when designing societies for an increasingly digital world.
